# Prostitution - A frank discussion

> I think it’s time we have candid, honest conversations about prostitution. While not everyone thinks it’s ideal, consensual adult sex work is not the same as exploitation or abuse (such as with minors or within families). Our policies and conversations should focus on protecting people from coercion, abuse, and trafficking, rather than further stigmatizing or criminalizing adults who engage in consensual sex work. Sometimes, focusing on what’s “moral” distracts us from what’s actually harmful. How can we better separate these issues—and what do we really want our laws and social policies to accomplish?

**1. Differentiating Issues**
- Not all illegal or taboo sexual acts are the same. Prostitution between consenting adults is fundamentally different—legally, ethically, and practically—from abuse or exploitation (e.g., incest or sex with a minor).
- Consent and agency are core concepts: adult prostitution generally involves personal choice (despite economic or social pressures), while abuse or exploitation never does.

**2. Why Prostitution is Stigmatized**
- Cultural and religious beliefs, historical legal frameworks, and concerns about exploitation or trafficking shape the stigma.
- Sex work is often conflated with non-consensual acts, when in reality, many sex workers are autonomous adults making choices.

**3. The Harm Reduction Perspective**
- Criminalizing prostitution tends to increase risks for sex workers, pushing issues underground and making it harder for them to seek help or protection.
- Decriminalization and/or legalization in some countries (e.g., New Zealand, parts of Australia, Netherlands) has reduced harm to sex workers and allowed for better regulation and health outcomes.

**4. Addressing Real Harms**
- Open public discussion allows society to direct resources and energy towards combating real harms: trafficking, abuse, exploitation of minors, and coercion.
- Sensible policies also help differentiate those who are choosing sex work from those who are victims of crime.

**5. Ethical and Moral Considerations**
- Some people’s opposition is rooted in personal or religious morality, which can coexist with supporting safety and rights for sex workers.
- Separating personal views from policy recommendations is important in public debate.
