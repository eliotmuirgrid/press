# Extremes

Pushing to extremes always changes the landscape. It brings not just dramatic progress, but also uncertainty and risk, because old assumptions no longer hold.

The classic example of course in [physics with relativity and quantum mechanics](physics.md).

But other examples exist in everyday life.



I’ve seen this firsthand in my own company. At one point, we had 20 people doing what, with the right automation, should have taken only one. By tearing down that inefficient structure—despite the short-term pain of reduced revenue and layoffs—we built something leaner and far more effective. Operating out of a tax-neutral jurisdiction like the Cayman Islands, our overall business performance jumped.
