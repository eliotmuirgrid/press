# The Machine

**This is a book by Justin Roff-Marsh**

![](machine.jpg)

Justin Roff-Marsh describes the application of Eliyahu Goldratt's [Theory of Constraints](/system/toc) to the sales and marketing function.

I was in the audience at a Peerscale meeting—an organization in Canada filled with very conventional sales leaders—when Justin gave a talk about rebuilding the sales function using what I only later learned was the Theory of Constraints.

His talk caused a surprising emotional uproar among the many sales leaders in the organization.

Personally, as a very rational and technical person deeply interested in ideas, I never felt that comfortable in Peerscale. The organization seemed very conventional in its thought processes and, when it came down to it, its members were more followers than leaders.

Justin's ideas were brilliant. Most sales leaders accept received wisdom without critically considering whether these things are actually true.

Let's take one popular statement of received "Sales Wisdom":

> There are hunters and farmers.

This gets recited like a religious mantra. It's the idea that the act of prospecting for new accounts should be done by a group of people called 'hunters', while the work of growing an account should be done by 'farmers'. Sadly, not everyone in the sales field is curious or intellectually gifted, and their thought process has not gone that deep.

But if you think more clearly, this is just one special case of a broader systems principle: systems become simpler and more efficient when you use the division of labor, which is itself the application of a powerful systems principle. By separating concerns, you can make a system simpler and more efficient.

Justin starts the book by talking about a theoretical event—a catastrophe in which the entire sales and marketing team perishes in a tragic accident. Perhaps that's why the sales leaders didn't like his talk. Did they take that idea personally?

Justin then outlines how the critical functions of the organization have to be rebuilt from scratch.
