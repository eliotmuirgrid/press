# Busy

Most people are too busy earning a living to think seriously about how to make money. The system consumes the very resource they need to redesign their relationship with it: **time to think**.

This is a powerful form of control because it doesn't necessarily require force. Communist systems have historically demonstrated an extreme version: when daily life is consumed by work, bureaucracy, shortages, and navigating the system, there is less capacity left to question the system itself. But the principle isn't unique to communism. Businesses and modern economies can produce the same effect simply through incentives and constant demands on attention.

Family systems can work this way too. **Homeostasis** keeps everyone playing familiar roles and responding to familiar problems. The recurring crisis becomes the thing that prevents anyone from examining why the crisis keeps recurring.

In **Theory of Constraints**, perhaps the deepest constraint is sometimes not money, people, or technology. It is **attention**. If a system keeps you permanently busy working *in* it, you never have enough capacity to work *on* it.

**Sometimes the most important productive thing you can do is create enough empty space to think.**
