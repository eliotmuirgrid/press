# Appearances

**Appearances can be deceiving when it comes to living the Theory of Constraints.**

Most people don't understand what it's like to live by the Theory of Constraints.

The Theory of Constraints isn't about finding the perfect solution. It's about iteration.

Identify the bottleneck. Apply the smallest effective change. Measure the result. Repeat.

Every constraint you remove reveals the next one. Progress isn't driven by massive, sweeping plans—it's driven by relentlessly eliminating the one thing that's holding everything else back.

In the beginning, an organization following this approach often looks like a complete disaster. Systems are changing, processes are being rewritten, roles are disappearing, and priorities shift constantly.

Don't be fooled.

If the organization is genuinely following the Theory of Constraints, you'll see rapid, measurable progress. Each bottleneck removed makes the next improvement easier, and the pace accelerates.

If it's only paying lip service, you'll see endless planning, endless meetings, and very little actually changes.

I don't just apply this to business—I live this way.

Right now, I'm choosing not to date. Not because I don't want to, but because I don't yet have the systems in place to make that a safe and sustainable choice. Adding complexity before removing the existing bottlenecks would be a mistake.

Instead, I'm investing in myself.

I've built routines that make looking after my health almost automatic. I write while I'm in the bathroom because it's otherwise unused time. After the gym, I swim in the ocean. Salt water washes away sweat, then I rinse off with fresh water. It's simple, efficient, and keeps me healthy with very little friction.

From the outside, my life can probably look unusual. That's fine.

I'm not trying to optimize everything. I'm optimizing the constraint.

That's what living the Theory of Constraints looks like. It's all about iterating.
