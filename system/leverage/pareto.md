# Pareto 

**Often known as the 80-20 rule**

The **Pareto Principle**, often called the **80-20 rule**, is a concept suggesting that in many situations, roughly 80% of consequences come from 20% of causes. Named after Italian economist Vilfredo Pareto, who observed that 80% of Italy’s land was owned by 20% of the population, this idea has been widely applied to business, economics, and everyday life.

For example, in business, it’s common to find that 80% of a company’s sales come from 20% of its customers. Similarly, in project management, managers might notice that 80% of problems arise from 20% of sources. The principle helps people focus on the most impactful tasks or issues, making it a valuable tool for prioritization and resource allocation.

It’s important to note that the 80-20 split is not a strict, mathematically exact ratio, but a general guideline. The actual numbers might be 70-30, 90-10, etc., but the underlying concept remains: a minority of causes, inputs, or effort often leads to a majority of the results or outcomes. Recognizing the Pareto Principle can help individuals and organizations be more efficient by identifying where to focus their attention for the greatest return

For many companies the figure might be more like 99% of the value comes from 1%.
