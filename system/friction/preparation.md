# Preparation 

**Standardize for Resilience**

One of the biggest mistakes an organization can make is accepting every component its suppliers provide. Every different screw, fitting, cable, software dependency, or proprietary part increases complexity and makes the organization more expensive and difficult to maintain.

Manufacturers optimize their products for manufacturing and sales—not for the long-term maintenance of your organization. Once you've bought the product, keeping it running becomes your responsibility.

Well-run organizations impose their own standards. Wherever practical, they replace proprietary hardware with standard components, reduce unnecessary variation, and build the internal knowledge needed to maintain their systems. The result is lower costs, faster repairs, and greater resilience.

This is especially important in healthcare.

Hospitals cannot afford to have critical equipment or clinical systems sitting idle because a specialized part is unavailable or because no one understands how to maintain the software. Every outage reduces capacity, disrupts patient care, and creates operational bottlenecks.

**COVID demonstrated how fragile global supply chains can become. Future disruptions—whether caused by geopolitical tensions, natural disasters, or rapid technological change—are inevitable.**

Healthcare organizations should be preparing now. Maintaining the capability to repair and support both their hardware and software infrastructure is not just an IT or facilities issue—it is a core business capability. The hospitals that standardize, simplify, and preserve maintenance knowledge will be the ones that continue delivering excellent patient care when others are waiting for parts.
