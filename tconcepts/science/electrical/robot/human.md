# Human

When people talk about robots, some get excited about the idea of a humanoid robot and are eager to wait for something like Tesla’s version—even if it might cost $30,000.

But is a humanoid robot with such a high price tag really the best solution? It doesn’t seem like a well-thought-out approach. This kind of thinking is an example of not going back to first principles and not being curious about what’s actually possible using cheaper, more readily available technology.

For instance, I can buy a $300 round robot that does a great job cleaning my pool. Using a humanoid robot for this—something that could be stolen or damaged much more easily—feels like a bigger risk and makes less sense.

You also have to think about parts and replacements. With more complex and expensive robots, those costs can add up and repairs get harder.

Personally, I’d rather make use of what’s available now than hold out for something that may never arrive or will likely have all sorts of built-in limitations designed so the company can maximize profit.
