# Heat

**A black, flat robot sitting in Cayman sun can have a significant heat problem.**

The combination is particularly unfavorable:

* **Black absorbs solar radiation** very efficiently.
* **Flat top = large surface directly facing the sun.**
* Cayman ambient temperatures around 30–33°C can translate into **surface temperatures of 50–70°C+** in direct sun.
* Batteries are usually the biggest concern. Lithium-ion batteries dislike sustained high temperatures; heat accelerates degradation and, at sufficiently high temperatures, the robot should shut itself down.
* Electronics, cameras/LiDAR, motors, plastics and adhesives can also suffer.

The important distinction is **air temperature vs. solar temperature**. A robot rated for operation at, say, 40°C ambient might be perfectly happy in 32°C shade but get considerably hotter sitting motionless on dark paving at 1 pm.

For Cayman, I'd make a simple rule: **robot works early morning/evening and parks in permanent shade.** A little ventilated docking shelter would probably solve most of the issue. A white or reflective cover over the parking location would help enormously too.
