# Base

## What’s a Base?
A **base** is just a type of chemical. But what makes it special is what it does when you mix it with water. You might have heard of acids—like lemon juice or vinegar. [Acids](acid.md) make things sour and can eat away stuff.

## Bases Do the Opposite 
A base does almost the opposite of an acid. When you put a base in water, it releases something called **OH⁻ ions** (that’s "oh minus" or "hydroxide ions").  
- Acids release **H⁺ ions** (hydrogen ions), making things acidic.
- Bases release **OH⁻ ions**, making things **less acidic** or even **soapy**.

## What Happens to [pH](water/ph)?  
[pH](water/ph) is just a number we use to tell if something is acidic or basic:
- Low pH (below 7): Acidic (sour, like lemon juice)
- High pH (above 7): Basic (soapy, like cleaning products)
- pH 7: Neutral (like pure water)

When a base is added to water, it *increases* the pH, making it more basic (NOT acidic) and less sour.

## Examples of Bases:
- Soap, Baking soda, Bleach

## Easy way to remember:  
**Acid** = "adds H⁺" = *makes water more sour*  
**Base** = "adds OH⁻" = *makes water more slippery/soapy*

## Summary

A base is just a chemical that, when put in water, makes the water less acidic (not more!) by adding OH⁻ ions, and makes the pH number go **up**.

*Fun fact:* If you touch soap and it’s slippery, it’s because it’s a base!
